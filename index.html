What’s newDust removal

Dust removal. New in Detail: tap Add spots, then tap each speck of sensor dust. Each one is healed the way darktable does it, by copying a matching patch from nearby and blending its edge into the surroundings, so sky stays smooth and texture stays real. Show dust makes specks easy to find. Where a spot sits across an edge, Other source or Choose picks a better patch. Spots stay for the next photo, since dust sits in the same place on the sensor, and they heal at full resolution on export.

Orton glow. New in Detail: the Orton effect, a blurred and brightened copy laid over the sharp photo for a dreamy glow that keeps the detail. Amount sets how strong, Softness how wide the glow spreads. It keeps the photo’s overall brightness, so dark infrared frames don’t sink.

Predict removed. The Predict from your filters card is gone. IR leak removal stays in Looks. To record your filter stack in exported files, pick the filters in Export, beside that option.

Predict, checked on your photo. Predict from your filters now compares its prediction with your photo’s own source channels (bars side by side) and says if the two clearly disagree, for example when the wrong filter is selected. It ends with numbered steps that actually use it: apply the swap, pick grey, and turn on IR leak removal when the stack calls for it.

Easier filter picker. Predict from your filters has a proper search: results appear as you type and a tap adds the filter, so you can build a stack of several. It finds filters without the exact name (“tiffen12” finds Wratten 12, which is the Tiffen numbering). The answer now comes first in plain words, what each channel records and the swap to use, with the numbers folded underneath. New example stacks: 720nm infrared, UV only and Aerochrome-style.

Kodak CC magenta. All seven Kodak colour-compensating magenta filters (CC025M to CC50M) are in the filter database, from Kodak’s own transmittance table and WRATTEN 2 chart.

Swap buttons in Simple. The channel swap buttons (R↔B and the rest) now show in Simple mode too. The mix grid and its fine controls stay in Advanced.

Hoya colour glass. Twelve more Hoya glass plates, Y44 to R72, from Hoya’s own datasheet tables, so the whole yellow-to-deep-red range is real data. The Hoya R70 card now uses the R70 glass table instead of an estimate. The Simple / Advanced switch now looks like the editor’s other buttons.

Hoya filters, real curves. Hoya’s Y (K2), YA3 and R (25A) cards now use curves traced from Hoya’s own 2016 catalogue chart instead of estimates. YA3 moved the most: its cut-on is about 563nm, not 540nm. Also new: the Hoya Y48 480nm glass plate, from Hoya’s own datasheet table, and a note on the X1 card that two Hoya charts disagree on its peak.

IR leak removal. New in Looks, for stacks that block blue (Aerochrome style): the blue channel then records pure infrared, and some infrared leaks into red and green too. This takes the leak back out before the swap, the method of the RYE converter discussed on ultravioletphotography.com. It works straight away from an estimate (the sensor curve and your filter stack); for your own camera’s exact figures, open one photo shot through an 850nm filter and press Measure: once per camera, remembered by model. On RAW files it is applied in the camera’s own raw space. Also: a digital Aerochrome stack is a one-tap example in Predict from your filters, the Hoya O(G) card now says its cut-on is disputed (510 or about 538nm), and the B+W 099 card notes that older 099s used a yellower glass.

Simple and Advanced. A switch at the top of the controls. Simple shows the tools most photos need; Advanced adds the channel matrix, filter prediction, local adjustments, HSL, calibration, swap space, LUTs, tone mapping, hotspot and fringe removal, the curve’s Points editor, decode settings and batch. Switching only hides tools, never resets them: if an Advanced setting is still affecting the photo in Simple, a note under the switch says which, and tapping it shows them. New visitors start in Simple; if you have used irlab before you start in Advanced.

Fix: empty Light panel. Opening a photo while on the Light or Detail tab closed that tab’s section, so the panel showed only its header until something reopened it. It now stays open.

Redesigned Points tab. Channels are one segmented control (RGB, R, G, B) with Reset beside it; the curve sits over the photo’s histogram for the channel you are editing; the selected point has its In and Out values and a delete button; presets and Luminance only are pills underneath.

Tone curve: Regions and Points. The tone curve opens on four sliders, Highlights, Lights, Darks and Shadows, that bend the curve for you on RGB or any one channel, with a small preview of the shape. They can never fold the curve back on itself. Points has the full point editor as before, and the two work together: the regions are applied first, shown as a dashed line behind your points.

Smaller tone curve on phones. It took up most of the controls; it is now a centred square no taller than about a third of the screen. The graph also sits slightly inside its box, so points at black, white or the top and bottom edges are drawn whole and are easy to grab.

Audit fixes. Every slider was swept on a grainy test frame. Dehaze called mid-greys half haze, so strong settings sent mid-tones to black (darker and brighter tones survived) and drew dark halos round trees; it now follows the dark-channel method properly and can no longer push a pixel through to black. Clarity treated sensor grain as local contrast and roughly doubled the grain of a noisy sky; it now compares a small area with its surroundings. The Foliage (IR) mask reads its index from the area round each pixel, so its edge no longer flickers on grainy frames. Everything else returns exactly to the original when reset, and nothing else produced black pixels or speckle beyond what its job is (sharpening and noise reduction work on fine detail by design).

Smooth Targeted colour and HSL Luminance. Targeted colour now decides which pixels it owns from the colour of the area around each one, not each pixel’s own grainy colour, so moving Lightness, Hue or Saturation on a noisy sky no longer breaks it into speckle: +2 EV on a picked sky now matches +2 EV of Exposure on it, with no extra grain. HSL Luminance now works like exposure too (±100 is ±2 stops), keeping contrast and saturation.

Picking a dim, greyish colour now selects it. Targeted colour chose pixels by hue, and a nearly grey colour (a dark sky in a raw file, before the swap) has no real hue, so a pick there selected nothing: the selection view stayed black and the sliders did nothing. Such picks now select by closeness in colour instead, with Width setting how loose. Earlier bands are unchanged.

Targeted colour Lightness works like exposure. It used to add the same amount of lightness to every part of the picked colour, which washed out its shadows and flattened its texture. Now it multiplies the light, as Exposure does, so the selection keeps its contrast and saturation; the slider reads in stops (±2 EV) and rolls off gently near white instead of clipping. Looks saved with a Lightness setting will render a little differently.

Tools snap into place. On a phone, opening a tool card (Temperature & tint, HSL, Colour calibration and so on) scrolls it to the top of the controls, so the whole tool is in view without scrolling. If you had pulled the controls down low to see more of the photo, they come back up to their normal height.

Sliders stop closing the panel. With the controls scrolled to the top, a touch beside a slider (next to the track or the − and + buttons) could start pulling the panel shut. Slider rows now count as controls, and the panel only follows a deliberate downward pull on an empty part of it.

Looks that travel. Save look as .cube is now made by the same code that draws the preview, so it matches the screen: it used to leave out HSL, colour calibration, white point, brightness and vibrance. The file says what it holds and what a .cube cannot (sharpening, noise reduction, clarity, dehaze, masks); its tip explains how to make it a Lightroom profile. Exports, single and batch, now record the filter stack from Predict from your filters in the file’s metadata (XMP), so a photo carries how it was taken. The stack is remembered between visits.

Easier to read. Opening a RAW now shows which step it is on (reading, decoding, building the preview) and how long it has taken, for the whole decode. Before/after starts on your edit. Some controls have plainer names (Colour rebuild, Lift per channel, Wide or Standard swap space), with the technical term in the tip. Preset cards say what each preset does; any caveat is in its tip.

Foliage (IR) masks. Local adjustments have a fifth mask that selects leaves by how they reflect near-infrared — the same index as the NDVI view, measured before the swap — so it follows the foliage whatever colours you swap it to. Set the index range, or switch to NGB. Show mask now darkens everything outside the mask instead of painting it red, so it reads on red infrared frames.

Predict from your filters. Under Source channels: add the filters on your lens and it works out what each channel records (UV, visible, infrared), the grey-card balance to expect and a suggested swap with its reasoning. It uses a real converted Sony A7III measured by JMC Scientific Consulting Ltd (Dr Jonathan Crowther; J. Imaging, 2022) by default.

RAW files open sooner. The photo shows as soon as it is decoded; the full-size copy and the camera-colour match are built afterwards. A RAW you open is read into memory straight away, which fixes “RAW sensor decode failed … permission problems” on Android, and a restored session keeps the RAW itself.

Filter data, sourced. Every filter in the database now names its source, the document and how its curve was taken from it, with a badge for what the data is (maker data, measured, chart, fit or estimate). SCHOTT glass now comes from SCHOTT’s own tables, which corrected 32 SCHOTT filters that read about 8 points too bright.

90 Edmund Optics filters. Longpass, shortpass, IR-cut, hot and cold mirrors, bandpass, machine-vision, astronomy and colour filters chosen for photography, each with Edmund’s own curve. Search “Edmund” to see them. The dichroic colour filters (red, green, blue, cyan, magenta, yellow) are measured only to about 780nm, so what they do further into the infrared is not known.

221 Thorlabs filters. The filter database now has Thorlabs’ hard-coated longpass, shortpass and bandpass filters from 289 to 1100nm, each with its measured curve from Thorlabs’ own data, which makes 500+ filters in all. Search “Thorlabs” to see them.

Sharing. Under Export Image: Share sends the photo to your phone’s share sheet, for Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and the rest. Before | after makes one picture of the original and your edit. Look link copies a link to your settings; whoever opens it gets the look on their next photo, kept in My presets.

Small fixes. Pop-up messages are translated too, and on a phone a long press on an icon in the top bar shows its name.

Trichrome. “› stack three shots” on the home page turns three real photos into one, each feeding one colour. Red Aerochrome rebuilds colour infrared film from an infrared, a red-filter and a green-filter shot: red foliage, deep blue skies. Also classic RGB trichrome, three infrared bands, or plain shots with no filters. The shots are lined up for you on the parts that did not move, then open in the editor.

Language on the filters page. The filter database has its own language menu at the top of its Data tab, shared with this one, so it can be changed there too.

Filters page translated. The filter database follows the language you pick here: its controls, band headings, filter descriptions, chart and data summary. Its long research notes stay in English.

Fully translated editor. Every label, note and tooltip in the editor is now in Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Chinese, not just the newer parts. This change log stays in English.

No grain pop. While a slider is held, the preview now shows the same grain as the finished frame, so noise no longer seems to appear the moment you let go.

Easier to tell apart. Reset all is now an eraser in the top bar, so it no longer looks like the rotate arrows next to it. On a phone the filter database opens in the same window, and your photo and edits are there when you come back.

Polish pass. Everything added this week is now in all six languages. The decode choices fold into one line on the home page, and come first, because opening a photo goes straight to the editor. Desktop matches the phone: square buttons, the IR badge, plain section headings. Labels are larger and quiet text has more contrast.

Open a photo from the prompt. The home page’s big button is now a small command prompt: tap “› open photo”, or drop a file. It shows which RAW decoder is set, and says when it is reading.

Calmer phone layout. Section headers lose their number chips and capitals; Reset (and Light’s Auto) sit in the header as small buttons instead of a row of their own; the decode details moved off the photo into Export › Decode settings; selected toggles are a tint instead of a solid orange pill.

New phone top bar. On a phone the controls above the photo are one row of eight labelled icons (New, Home, Reset, Left, Right, Full, Theme, Filters), so they all fit on any screen.

New button style. Choose image, New image and Export are now dark keys with an orange chip, like the numbered steps, instead of the glowing orange buttons. On a phone New image shortens to fit the top bar on one line.

Cleaner Channel hue. Hue shift in Channel mode now keeps each pixel’s brightness, so big turns on a noisy sky no longer come out speckled. 120° still matches Cyclic rotation, as it looks with Hold brightness ticked.

Cards everywhere. Looks, Light and Export now use the same cards as Colour and Detail, each with a dot when something in it is changed. White Balance is one slim card: two rows with a dropper and a clear button, gains folded underneath, and Temperature & tint in its own card.

Compact channel mix. The swap grid is about half the height. Tap a cell to fine-tune it on one bar (tap again to type), the shading shows how strong each route is, and offsets fold away until you need them.

Tidier Colour tab. Colour is now cards, like Detail: Overall (hue, saturation, vibrance) open at the top, then Colour by band, Calibration, Swap working space and LUT, with a dot on the ones in use. The White Balance header says what is set even when it is closed.

Clearer white balance. The two grey pickers are now numbered cards, before the swap and after it, each with its own status. Auto tone's channel gains fold away under one line that still shows their values.

New logo. IR in an orange ring, on the tab icon, the home-screen icon and the home page.

Tidier Detail tab. Detail is now a short list of cards in order of importance: Hotspot removal first (with its own Set centre), then Noise (RAW, regular and banding together), Sharpening, Clarity & dehaze, Tone mapping and Colour fringing. A dot shows which cards are doing something. Vignette has been removed.

No more blotchy skies between two bands. A sky that sits between Blue and Purple used to go patchy when only one of the two was darkened. Each pixel's band is now chosen from the colour of a wide area around it (without crossing edges), so the whole sky moves together.

Full-size view shows the real grain. Fitted to the screen, the full-size view now averages the picture down properly instead of letting the browser skip pixels, which made skies look about twice as grainy as the file really is. Zoom in and it switches to the full-size pixels. Colour and luminosity masks also choose their area from smoothed colour, so they no longer flicker pixel to pixel on a noisy sky.

Colour by band no longer speckles. Darkening a band (a sky, say) used to scatter dark dots through it on noisy photos, because each pixel picked its band from its own noisy colour. Bands are now chosen from the colour around each pixel without crossing edges, and Luminance darkens by a proportion, so shadows never get pushed to black.

AMaZE is back, next to RCD. Canon and other Bayer RAWs now decode with AMaZE by default, and RCD is one tap away in Decode settings. Switching re-decodes the photo you have open and keeps your edits, so the two can be compared directly.

Home page redesign. A cleaner lab-style home page: numbered Decode, Load and Tools sections (decode options first, so they are set before a photo opens), a framed load panel whose file slot glows while a photo is read, the spectrum bar lighting up block by block, and a filter chart that draws itself in.

Detail tools work together. Noise reduction now works on RAW files, sharpening no longer brings back the noise it removed, and Clarity only adds local contrast instead of brightening the whole photo. JPEGs also keep their deep shadows with no edits applied. Easier-to-read labels and larger checkboxes on phones.

Real sensor decoding for RAW. Canon and most other RAW files are now decoded from the sensor with RCD demosaicing (Fuji X-Trans with Markesteijn), instead of falling back to the camera’s small preview. Fixes the failure on newer Canons (600D, 7D, 1200D and others) and the washed-out look from an uncorrected black level.

RAWs open the right way up. Portrait shots are turned upright on loading, from the orientation the camera recorded, on phone and desktop.

Desktop: zoom with the mouse wheel. Scroll over the photo to zoom in where the pointer is, and back out. The decode and white balance readout now sits under the histogram instead of over the photo, and dragging the before/after slider no longer moves a zoomed-in photo.

Noise reduction holds at full size. Luminance and Colour noise reduction in Detail now look the same in full-screen view and full-size exports as they do in the preview.

Before shows your last change. The before/after slider and hold-to-compare now show the photo as it was just before your last edit, and follow Undo and Redo. Tap Before to switch it to the original.

New layouts. A desktop workspace with a tool rail, a large photo and one tab of controls at a time, in the same glass look as this page; the phone layout on an iPad held upright; this home page fitting one screen; and no accidental page zoom — only the photo zooms. Also new: a Widening slider under Swap Working Space, and presets that save more than once in a row.

Auto levels, and better RAW handling. A one-tap Auto levels in the Light panel (RawTherapee’s method). For RAW files: RawTherapee’s wavelet noise reduction and its inpaint-opposed highlight rebuild, both applied before any colour work. In Detail: RawTherapee’s edge-preserving tone mapping, which compresses big tonal differences without halos.

Removed to keep it simple: hue curves, the tri-tone merge, the wide-gamut preview, and the EIR, Aerochrome 1443 and CIR film stocks.

Perceptual: equal hue steps look equally different to the eye. Channel: matches how a channel swap moves colour, so 120° lines up with a 3-way swap like Cyclic rotation (with brightness held).

Swap working spacehow wide the swap works

The channel swap above runs in a colour space wider than what you see on screen, then converts back — a wider space holds more of a saturated colour's true position before the swap moves it, so more survives the trip back down. ProPhoto is the strongest version of this and the default; Rec2020 gives a milder lift for anyone who finds ProPhoto too much; sRGB disables the effect entirely, so the swap matches exactly what its numbers say with nothing else happening to the colour. Names in brackets are the darktable profiles with the same primaries and white point: sRGB and Rec.709 share both, so choosing sRGB here is the same as working in darktable’s linear Rec709 RGB.
