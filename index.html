Source channels — which one carries your signal?

The three channels of the image as loaded, before any swap. With a full-spectrum sensor behind a filter stack, which channel actually holds the IR or UV response depends on the stack, not on convention — so read it here rather than inferring it from the swapped result. The mean is the channel's average level: a channel sitting near black is mostly noise, and swapping it into a visible slot will amplify that noise.

Calibrate matrix from real patches

A preset is someone's guess about one filter stack under one light. This derives the matrix from measurement instead: photograph a few materials with stable, distinctive signatures under your own stack — PTFE or white card for a neutral, black velvet for a true zero, a couple of pigments — tap each one below, say what colour it should become, and solve. Three well-chosen patches is usually enough; more gives the solve room to average out noise in any one of them, rather than fitting each exactly. Uses white balance as it stands right now, so set that above first — change it afterward and the calibration below no longer matches what it was solved against.

A tight fit through very few, very different patches can demand an extreme gain on one channel — tap a shadow and ask it to become white, for instance — and that gain amplifies the source image's own noise right along with the colour, which is what shows up as heavy grain. Smoothing trades a little fit accuracy for a lot less of that. Raise it if a solve comes out grainy; 0 fits the patches exactly, for when you know that's what you want.

Each row's total is that channel's brightness: over 100% lifts it, under 100% drops it. Ticking the box scales each row back to 100%, changing colour without changing exposure. Rows already at 100% — every pure swap — are unaffected, so the box does nothing visible on those.

4. Finishing Controls

Some colours cannot be both bright and saturated — a full blue is a fraction as bright as a full yellow. At 0% those colours stay bright and go pale; at 100% they keep their richness and go darker. Hue is exact either way. Mostly affects hue shifts into blue and violet, and strong swaps.

Tri-tone merge

Three hue-rotated copies of the image, each auto-levelled on its own histogram, combined by taking the brightest of the three in each channel. A rebuild of the Aerochrome Photoshop action, whose look came from exactly this — layers at +118, +80 and +149 merged with Lighten. Defaults are the action's own values.

Split toning

Two tints crossing over between dark and bright. Added as chroma in OKLab, so it colours without lifting or dropping brightness. This is the one look a channel matrix cannot produce, because a matrix treats every pixel the same regardless of how bright it is.

Defringe removes the purple/magenta halos IR lenses leave on high-contrast edges. It smooths colour only — edge sharpness is unaffected. Start low; too much desaturates fine detail.
