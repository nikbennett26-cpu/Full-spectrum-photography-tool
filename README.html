01Opening photos

Drop a file on the page or tap Open photo. A photo opens at its full working size straight away.

Ordinary images

  • JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and TIFF.
  • 16-bit TIFF in, 16-bit TIFF out.

RAW files sensor decode

  • CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, SR2, DNG, ORF, RW2, PEF, RAF, SRW, NRW, 3FR, IIQ, MRW, KDC, DCR and X3F.
  • Decoded from the sensor data in your browser by LibRaw compiled to WebAssembly.
  • A quick-preview mode reads the camera's embedded JPEG instead.

Pick up where you left off

  • The photo and your edits are saved in the browser. Come back within 30 minutes and it reopens by itself; later, a Restore button offers it.
  • Undo and redo, and Reset all.

02The RAW pipeline

Work that is best done on the sensor data is done there, before the channel swap stretches every fault.

  1. Decode
  2. Hot pixels & stripes
  3. Highlight rebuild
  4. Noise reduction
  5. Deconvolution
  6. White balance
  7. Camera colour
  8. Channel swap & mix
  9. Edit
  10. Export

Demosaicing

  • AMaZE by default for Bayer sensors, with RCD one tap away. Switching re-decodes the open photo and keeps your edits, so the two can be compared.
  • Markesteijn for Fuji X-Trans.

White balance RAW

  • Opens on the camera's own as-shot balance.
  • Temperature in Kelvin and Green, using RawTherapee's model, with As shot, Auto and Pick.
  • Dragging runs on the graphics chip, so it follows your finger at full resolution.

Highlight recovery RAW

  • Rebuilds clipped highlights from the colours around them (the inpaint-opposed method), measured against where each channel really clips in your file.

Noise reduction RAW

  • RawTherapee's wavelet noise reduction, applied to the sensor data before the swap, where the noise is still as the sensor recorded it.
  • On by default when you open a RAW (Colour 50, Luminance 0, so texture stays). Whatever you set by hand, including off, is remembered.

Hot pixels & stripes RAW

  • A tick box that finds stuck or hot pixels and faint row or column stripes at full size and fixes them, reversibly.
  • Real points of light such as stars are left alone, and stripes are only touched when they can actually be found.

Wide-gamut swap & camera colour

  • Optionally do the swap in the camera's own wider colour space (or ProPhoto) so saturated colours survive it.
  • The sensor decode can be fitted to the camera's own JPEG rendering of the same frame.

03The editor

Five tabs, in the order the picture is made: Looks, Light, Colour, Detail, Export.

Simple or Advanced

Chosen on the home page. Simple shows what most photos need. Advanced adds the channel mix grid, IR leak removal, local adjustments, HSL, calibration, swap space, LUTs, Orton glow, channel gains and more.

Seeing your photo

Before and after slider, or hold to compare. Zoom and pan (mouse wheel on desktop, pinch on a phone), full-screen view, a histogram and RGB parade, and clipping and print-gamut warnings.

Made for a phone

A phone dock with controls under your thumb, long-press a slider value to type a number, double-tap to reset it, and haptic taps. A desktop workspace gives a tool rail and a large photo.

Honest display

Where the browser allows it, the preview is 10-bit to avoid colour banding while you edit. Elsewhere it falls back to 8-bit with dither, and Export says which one you have.

04Looks

Where the colours are chosen.

Swap & mix

  • One-tap swaps: R↔B, G↔B, R↔G and the cyclic rotations.
  • A full channel mix grid with offsets, and a compact fine-tune bar for each cell.
  • Hold brightness or keep saturation while you swap.

Presets & film recipes

  • UV and IR presets, and recipes including Fedia's Triple Band Aerochrome.
  • Save your own looks, with a strength control and an IR tone curve.
  • NDVI and NGB index views.

Source channels

  • A readout of which source channel carries your signal.
  • For filter stacks that block blue, IR leak removal takes the infrared out of red and green before the swap, for cleaner Aerochrome-style colour.

Temperature & tint

  • Camera white balance (RAW), then a balance before the swap and one after it, each with a grey picker.
  • Auto tone and channel gains.
  • Finer control where real settings sit: half the slider travel covers ±100.

05Light

Exposure, tone and where black and white sit.

Exposure & tone

  • Exposure, brightness, contrast, highlights and shadows.
  • Highlights and Shadows read an edge-aware map of the picture's brightness, so a sky is lifted or recovered as one area, with no glow along its edge.
  • Range controls set where highlights and shadows start.
  • One-tap Auto levels (RawTherapee's method).

Black & white point

  • Set where black and white sit, with a fine taper near zero.

Tone curve

  • Regions: four sliders (Highlights, Lights, Darks, Shadows) bend the curve for you.
  • Points: drag points on RGB or on each channel, over that channel's histogram, with presets.

Local adjustments

  • Up to four, each with a linear, radial, luminosity, colour or foliage (IR) mask.
  • The foliage mask selects leaves by how they reflect near-infrared, so it follows them whatever colour you swap them to.

06Colour

Shaping the colours once they are swapped.

Overall

  • Hue, saturation and vibrance.
  • Hue shift keeps each pixel's brightness, so big turns on a noisy sky stay clean.

Colour by band

  • Eight bands, each with hue, saturation and luminance.
  • Bands are chosen from the colour of the area around each pixel, not its own grainy colour, so a sky moves as one.

Targeted colour Advanced

  • Pick a colour on the photo and shift its hue, saturation or lightness. Lightness works like exposure, so the selection keeps its contrast.
  • Applied to the original colours, before the swap.

Calibration, working space, LUTs Advanced

  • Primary and shadow-tint calibration.
  • Choose how wide the swap works: standard sRGB, Rec2020, Wide (ProPhoto, the default), or your own widening.
  • Apply a .cube LUT as a final grade.

07Detail

Cleaning and sharpening, in order of importance.

Hotspot removal

  • Takes out the bright centre patch some lenses throw on infrared, with a Set centre control.

Dust removal

  • Tap each speck and it is healed the way darktable does it, by copying a matching patch from nearby and blending its edge in.
  • Find dust looks for small, soft, round, darker spots on smooth areas such as sky and adds them for you. Sensitivity sets how faint a spot it takes; Show dust helps you check.

Noise

  • RAW luminance and colour noise reduction, hot pixels and stripes (RAW files).
  • Regular luminance and colour noise reduction that works on any photo.
  • Smooth banding melts the steps in a smooth sky after a strong edit.

Sharpening RAW

  • RAW deconvolution: real Richardson-Lucy deconvolution on the sensor data before the swap, giving back fine detail. Blur radius is measured from the photo (the method of ART) or set by hand. Fades out in noise and near clipped highlights. Amount 0 is off.
  • Amount, radius and masking, in the style of RapidRAW.
  • Sharpening does not bring back the noise that was removed.

Clarity & dehaze

  • Clarity adds local contrast only, without brightening the picture.
  • Dehaze measures haze across the whole picture (a dark-channel haze map), so a sky clears evenly right up to a dark building, with no halo.

Orton glow & colour fringing

  • The Orton effect: a blurred, brightened copy over the sharp photo, with Amount and Softness.
  • Fringing controls for coloured edges toward the corners.

08Export

What you get out is what you saw on screen.

Files

  • Crop to common aspect ratios.
  • 16-bit TIFF, PNG or JPEG, at working size or full size, with optional ProPhoto RGB.
  • Your EXIF and colour profile carry across.

Batch

  • Apply the current look to many files at once and download them in one .zip. RAWs are exported at full size.

Take your look with you

  • Save look as .cube, made by the same code that draws the preview.
  • Save look for camera (.IRL) for a Canon running Magic Lantern with the irlut module, so you see the look in LiveView.
  • Look link: your settings packed into a web address anyone can open.

Share

  • Send a full-quality JPEG to your phone's share sheet (Instagram, WhatsApp and the rest).
  • Before | after makes one picture of the original and your edit.

09Trichrome

Stack three real shots into one, each feeding one colour. Start it from the home page ("stack three shots").

Recipes

Red Aerochrome (an infrared, a red-filter and a green-filter shot rebuilt as colour infrared film), classic RGB filter trichrome, three infrared bands, and plain shots with no filters.

Alignment

Shots are lined up automatically on the parts that did not move (shift and small rotation, with a hand nudge), balanced, and opened in the editor as a 16-bit TIFF.

10Filter database

A separate page, irlab.uk/filters.html, for working out what your lens stack lets through.

500+ filters

  • Schott glass, B+W, Hoya, Wratten, Kodak colour-compensating, 221 Thorlabs filters and 90 Edmund Optics filters, most with digitised or measured manufacturer curves.
  • Search by name, or by what you want it to do (UV-pass, IR-pass, IR blocker, colour).

Stack and predict

  • Stack several filters to see the combined transmission.
  • See the predicted RGB response, and check a stack for infrared leaks.
  • Suggest a recipe: describe the look you want and get filter combinations.

Data you can trust

  • Every filter names its source and how the curve was taken from it, with a badge: maker data, measured, chart, fit or estimate.
  • A data self-test checks every curve, and you can switch to real data only.

Bring and take

  • Import your own filter data.
  • Export CSV, JSON or a PNG report of your stack.

12Development

Plain JavaScript and WebGL2, no framework. See README.md for the source layout in full.

Edit src/, never editor.js

tools/build.py joins the sources into the one script each page loads. python3 tools/bump_build.py <build> sets the build number everywhere and rebuilds.

Tests

run.mjs, cases.mjs and golden.json compare the build with a stored baseline on software WebGL. The in-app self-test checks rules that must always hold.
