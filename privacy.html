Privacy

For irlab.uk, its filter page, and the IR Lab app for Android. Last updated 2 November 2026.

Your photos stay on your device

Opening, decoding, editing and saving photos all happen on your phone or computer. Your photos are never uploaded, and nobody, including us, can see them. This also applies to photos you share into the app from your gallery.

What is kept on your device

The app remembers your settings in your browser's local storage: things like your language, the filters you said were on your lens, and which panels you had open. It also keeps a copy of the app itself so it works offline. None of this is sent anywhere. Clearing the site's data (or uninstalling the Android app) removes it.

Visit counting

The site uses Google Analytics to count visits, so we can see roughly how many people use it and which pages they open. It is set up with IP addresses anonymised, and with Google signals and ad personalisation turned off. Google Analytics sets cookies and receives standard browser details such as device type, browser, approximate location (country or city level) and the pages you view. It never receives your photos. A notice tells you this on your first visit, and the "Cookie notice" link at the bottom of the app shows it again. Google's own policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

Other things the page loads

  • Fonts from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address when it sends them.
  • When you check for an update, the app asks irlab.uk which build is current.

The app has no accounts and no adverts, and doesn't sell or share data with anyone.

The Android app

IR Lab for Android shows irlab.uk full-screen in Chrome (or your default browser), so everything above applies to it in the same way. It asks for no special permissions beyond internet access, and photos only reach it when you pick or share them.
